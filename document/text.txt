Ancient cloud studies were not made in isolation, but were observed in
combination with other weather elements and even other natural sciences.
Around 340 BC, Greek philosopher Aristotle wrote Meteorologica, a work which
represented the sum of knowledge of the time about natural science, including
weather and climate. For the first time, precipitation and the clouds from which 
precipitation fell were called meteors, which originate from the Greek word meteoros,
meaning 'high in the sky'. From that word came the modern term meteorology, the study
of clouds and weather. Meteorologica was based on intuition and simple observation,
but not on what is now considered the scientific method. Nevertheless, it was the
first known work that attempted to treat a broad range of meteorological topics
in a systematic way, especially the hydrological cycle.
